  <p>
Over recent decades, party system fragmentation has increased, but research on its effects has not followed the same trend. In this paper, I examine the impact of fragmentation on the electoral outcome of clientelistic parties and their use of clientelistic practices.  Using an instrumental variable approach that exploits the quasi-random variation introduced by electoral thresholds, I analyze local-level elections in Colombia. The findings reveal that increased fragmentation negatively affects clientelistic parties' electoral success. increased fragmentation significantly reduces the vote share, seat share, mayoral success, and candidate presence of clientelistic parties. Mechanism analysis suggests that fragmentation undermines voter trust in parties and weakens partisan identification, thereby reducing the credibility and effectiveness of clientelistic exchanges. On the supply side, it heightens intra-party competition within clientelistic networks, leading to more mobilization but lower efficiency. However, while fragmentation discourages clientelism while benefiting group-representing and charismatic leaders, this does not necessarily translate into more programmatic politics or improved public goods provision.
